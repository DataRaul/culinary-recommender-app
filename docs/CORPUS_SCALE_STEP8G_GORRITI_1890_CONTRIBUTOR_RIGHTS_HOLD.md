## Documentary review

The source itself is an 1890 Buenos Aires edition published by Félix Lajouane. Independent bibliographic records identify the same title, author, place, publisher and year.

Juana Manuela Gorriti died in 1892, so her own authorial exploitation term is long expired under the project's Spain-facing historical-term framework.

However, the exact book is not simply a single-author cookbook. Contemporary/research descriptions identify it as a community compilation made from recipes sent by a large network of women in Hispano-America, with individual recipe contributions attributed in the work. Scholarship on the original 1890 text describes roughly 177 women contributing approximately 244 recipes.

Spain's current Copyright Act preserves the duration provided by the 1879 Act for authors who died before 7 December 1987, and Article 6 of the 1879 Act provided an eighty-year post-mortem term. The project does not have contribution-level death/rights evidence for the full contributor set.

Therefore the project cannot infer that every potentially protectable contributed textual expression in the exact source is outside the applicable Spain-facing term.

## Decision

The exact source key is **held**. The ORA `public-domain` marker is not sufficient to clear the contributor layer.

This is a provenance/rights hold, not a finding that the historical recipes or culinary facts themselves are protected. The project may reconsider the cohort if a bounded documentary audit establishes a legally sufficient contribution-level basis or a source representation that avoids the unresolved expressive layer under the project's admission rules.

No source-specific measurement, prewrite, D1 population, public activation or recommendation admission is earned from this source while the hold remains.
