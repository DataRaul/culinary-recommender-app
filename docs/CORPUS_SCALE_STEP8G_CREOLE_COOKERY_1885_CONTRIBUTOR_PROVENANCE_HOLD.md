## Exact work / edition identity

Open Library maps Internet Archive item `creolecookerybo00unkngoog` to the 1885 New Orleans edition published by T.H. Thomason. Independent bibliographic records describe the Christian Woman's Exchange as **editor**, not as a single natural-person recipe author.

The title-page description states that the recipes are contributions of housekeepers experienced in Southern and Creole cookery. Historical documentation of the same 1885 work describes a multi-contributor collection associated with the Exchange.

## Material attribution / provenance ambiguity

The pinned ORA representation assigns the cohort the single record-level author value **"Christian Woman's Exchange"**.

That flattens two distinct roles:

1. the Exchange's editorial/compilation role; and
2. the underlying recipe-contributor layer documented by the work itself.

The exact representation does not preserve recipe-by-recipe contributor identity or a defensible boundary separating editorial selection from the contributed culinary matter. The source history further indicates that at least some material entered through layered oral/household traditions rather than a single identified writer.

This is a material provenance issue under the Legal Corpus First admission contract. The project does not need to decide ownership of historical culinary facts or ideas to fail closed on an exact reusable representation whose attribution model is materially incomplete.
